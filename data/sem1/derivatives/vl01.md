# Derivatives · VL 01 – Introduction

### What defines a derivative?
- [ ] A financial instrument that is always traded on an exchange and centrally cleared
- [x] A financial instrument whose value depends on the value of other, more basic underlying variables
- [ ] A financial instrument that guarantees a fixed payoff at maturity
- [ ] A security issued by a company to raise capital, whose value depends on the firm's assets
> Explanation: A derivative derives its value from an underlying variable (stock, commodity, interest rate, ...). It can be exchange traded or OTC, and payoffs are generally uncertain.

### Which functions do derivatives fulfil in financial markets? (multiple answers)
- [x] Hedging of risk (risk transfer)
- [x] Levered speculation on price movements (risk acquisition)
- [x] Arbitrage and price discovery
- [ ] Guaranteed elimination of all market risk for both counterparties
> Explanation: The three economic functions are hedging, levered speculation, and arbitrage/price discovery. Derivatives transfer risk between parties; they do not eliminate it for both sides — futures are a zero-sum game.

### Which statements apply to exchange-traded derivatives (as opposed to OTC derivatives)? (multiple answers)
- [x] Contracts are standardized by the exchange
- [x] There is virtually no credit risk due to central clearing
- [ ] Instruments are traded bilaterally between two parties
- [ ] There is no standardization and historically no central clearing
> Explanation: Exchange-traded derivatives are standardized and centrally cleared, so credit risk is virtually eliminated. Bilateral trading without standardization characterizes the OTC market — although since the financial market reforms a large part of OTC trades is subject to mandatory reporting and central clearing.

### What is the role of a Central Counterparty (CCP)?
- [ ] It matches buyers and sellers and earns the bid-ask spread
- [ ] It guarantees a minimum return on derivatives positions
- [x] It steps in as counterparty to each side of a trade, so there is no direct bilateral exposure between market participants
- [ ] It sets the prices of derivatives contracts on behalf of the exchange
> Explanation: A CCP becomes buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer. This removes bilateral counterparty exposure and is the standard clearing procedure for exchange-traded derivatives; because of systemic risk concerns, central clearing is now also mandatory for a large part of OTC derivatives.

### How do the OTC and exchange-traded derivatives (ETD) markets compare in size (BIS, June 2025)?
- [ ] ETD markets dominate, with outstanding notional roughly eight times larger than OTC
- [ ] Both markets have roughly the same outstanding notional
- [x] OTC markets dominate: global outstanding notional is just below $800 trn vs. more than $100 trn for ETD
- [ ] OTC outstanding notional is below $100 trn, ETD above $800 trn
> Explanation: According to BIS statistics the OTC market clearly dominates: OTC outstanding notional is just under $800 trillion, while ETD notional is somewhat above $100 trillion.

### Which features distinguish futures from forwards? (multiple answers)
- [x] Futures are settled daily, forwards at the end of the contract
- [x] Futures are standardized exchange contracts, forwards are private bilateral contracts
- [ ] Futures carry some credit risk, forwards virtually none
- [x] Futures usually end without delivery, forwards usually with delivery or cash settlement
> Explanation: Futures: exchange traded, standardized, daily settlement (marking to market), usually no delivery, virtually no credit risk. Forwards: private, non-standardized, settled at maturity, usually delivery or cash settlement, some credit risk. The credit risk statement is reversed.

### A future contract is signed at t=0 at price F₀. What is the value of this contract for both parties at inception, and why?
- [x] Zero – the future price is set such that the contract has no initial value
- [ ] Equal to the option premium the buyer pays at signing
- [ ] Equal to the spot price S₀, because the underlying must be delivered at maturity
- [ ] Positive for the long side if the market expects prices to rise
> Explanation: The future price is the predetermined delivery price chosen so that the contract has zero value at signing. No premium is paid (unlike options). Profits/losses arise later as a zero-sum game: profit of buyer = loss of seller (ST vs. F₀).

### An investor agreed to sell an asset via a forward at F₀ = 80. At maturity the spot price is ST = 73. What is the investor's profit or loss?
- [x] Profit of 7 per unit
- [ ] Loss of 7 per unit
- [ ] Profit of 73 per unit
- [ ] Zero – forwards have no payoff at maturity
> Explanation: The party that agreed to sell has a short position. The short gains when prices fall: F₀ − ST = 80 − 73 = +7. The long side loses exactly 7 (zero-sum game).

### What distinguishes an American from a European option?
- [ ] American options are traded in the US, European options in Europe
- [ ] American options are settled in cash, European options by physical delivery
- [x] An American option can be exercised at any time during its life, a European option only at maturity
- [ ] American options always have a higher strike price
> Explanation: The names refer only to exercise rights, not to geography or settlement. American: exercise any time until maturity. European: exercise only at maturity.

### Which statement on the difference between options and futures is correct?
- [ ] Both options and futures give the holder the right, but not the obligation, to trade
- [x] An option gives the right to buy or sell, while a future imposes an obligation; therefore the option buyer pays a premium while a future has no premium
- [ ] A future buyer pays a premium, while options are free to enter
- [ ] Options are zero-sum games, futures are not
> Explanation: A future/forward holder has the obligation to buy or sell at the agreed price; an option holder has the right. This asymmetry in payoffs is why the option buyer must pay an option premium, whereas no premium is exchanged when a future is opened.
