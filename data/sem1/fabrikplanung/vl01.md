# Fabrikplanung · VL 01 – Introduction

### Which developments characterize the four industrial revolutions in the correct order?
- [ ] 1.0 electrification → 2.0 mechanization → 3.0 IoT → 4.0 automation
- [x] 1.0 mechanization (water/steam) → 2.0 electrification (assembly line) → 3.0 automation (electronics/computers) → 4.0 cyber-physical systems and IoT
- [ ] 1.0 automation → 2.0 mechanization → 3.0 electrification → 4.0 digital twin
- [ ] 1.0 mechanization → 2.0 automation → 3.0 electrification → 4.0 artificial intelligence
> Explanation: Industry 1.0 = mechanization via water and steam power (first power loom 1784), 2.0 = electrically powered mass-production assembly lines (Ford/Taylor, ~1910), 3.0 = automation through electronics and computers (first PLC 1968), 4.0 = cyber-physical systems, IoT, cloud, AI toward smart production.

### What is the defining organizational principle of Taylorism ("Scientific Management")?
- [x] Consistent separation of planning and execution, with each worker specialized in a small portion of work
- [ ] Integration of planning and execution in autonomous work teams to increase motivation
- [ ] Maximizing product variety so each worker masters many different tasks
- [ ] Decentralizing all supervisory tasks down to the individual worker
> Explanation: Taylor separated planning from execution (symbolized by planners in glass booths above the shop floor). Workers were highly specialized on a few clocked tasks; product variety was deliberately limited, enabling mass production such as the single-variant Ford Model T.

### How is "automation" defined in the sense of the lecture?
- [ ] The complete elimination of humans from all factory operations
- [x] The transfer of human labor to machines based on technological progress
- [ ] The use of freely programmable robots exclusively for assembly tasks
- [ ] The digital mapping of all factory processes in virtual models
> Explanation: Automation is the transfer of human labor to machines enabled by technological progress. In fully automated plants machines do the manufacturing while humans typically take on supervisory tasks; it also enables processes infeasible for humans (high forces, hazardous environments).

### What best describes Industry 4.0?
- [ ] The introduction of electronics and stand-alone robots replacing manual work
- [x] The interlinking of production with information and communication technology, based on cyber-physical systems and the Internet of Things
- [ ] Labor-based mass production on electrically powered assembly lines
- [ ] The mechanization of production using water and steam power
- [ ] The consistent separation of planning and execution
> Explanation: Industry 4.0 interlinks production with ICT. Its technical foundation is cyber-physical systems, intelligent sensor networks and the digital twin, enabling largely self-organized, closed-loop production where people, machines, logistics and products communicate directly. (The other options describe earlier industrial stages.)

### Which statements about Computer-integrated manufacturing (CIM) are correct? (Mehrfachauswahl)
- [x] CIM is a collective term linking computer-supported activities (CAD, CAE, CAPP, PPC/ERP, MES, CAM, CAQ, ...) into one integrated information system
- [x] The concept was introduced in 1973 by economist Joseph Harrington, emphasizing the importance of information in production
- [x] The main early challenge was the interfaces for communication between the individual "isolated solutions"
- [ ] CIM systems were designed from the start to exchange data seamlessly among all components
> Explanation: CIM bundles all computer-aided activities from product idea to market launch into one integrated information system (Harrington, 1973). The components were initially used as "isolated solutions" without data exchange; in the early 1990s the interfaces between them were the biggest implementation hurdle. The Digital Factory is seen as an evolution of CIM.

### What does the term "digital twin" refer to?
- [ ] A backup copy of the ERP database used for disaster recovery
- [x] The effortless integration of data between a physical and a virtual machine in either direction
- [ ] A second identical physical machine kept as a redundant production resource
- [ ] A static CAD model of a machine created before commissioning
> Explanation: The digital twin denotes the seamless, bidirectional data integration between a physical asset and its virtual counterpart (CAD/FEA models, real-time operational and maintenance data, fleet data). The bidirectional, live data flow is what distinguishes it from a static CAD model.

### Which characteristics apply to cyber-physical systems (CPS)? (Mehrfachauswahl)
- [x] They contain embedded software and consist of sensors and actuators
- [x] They evaluate and store collected data and are interconnected via communication networks
- [x] They provide a human-machine interface
- [ ] They operate in complete isolation without any external network connection
> Explanation: CPS contain embedded software, sensors and actuators, evaluate/store data, are networked locally and globally, use and provide data/services to support virtual planning, and have a human-machine interface. When applied in production they are called cyber-physical production systems (CPPS) — isolation would contradict their networked nature.

### By which characteristics does a factory distinguish itself from a craft enterprise?
- [ ] Manufacturing of unique, individually commissioned pieces at the customer's residence
- [x] A larger number of workers outside their residence, highly segmented division of labor, organizational consolidation, and production of commercial goods for market sale
- [ ] Focus on a single worker's technical skills with few supporting machines
- [ ] Absence of division of labor to preserve craftsmanship quality
> Explanation: A factory (Latin fabrica – workshop) is a production facility for extraction, refining or processing of materials. It differs from craft/self-sufficient enterprises through a larger workforce (outside the home), highly segmented division of labor, organizational consolidation, and manufacturing of commercial products for the market.

### How does factory planning relate to the other areas of corporate planning?
- [ ] Factory planning is the starting point that determines the overall market strategy of the company
- [x] Factory planning is a subfield of corporate planning that builds on production planning and is constrained by investment planning
- [ ] Factory planning is independent of production and investment planning and follows only technical criteria
- [ ] Factory planning replaces strategic planning once a new plant is commissioned
> Explanation: Strategic planning is the starting point for all corporate planning. Factory planning is a subordinate subfield that builds on production planning (deciding how, what, in what quantities and where production takes place) while investment planning sets the budget constraints.

### Why is a factory regarded as an "open system" in the systems approach?
- [ ] Because its layout can be freely rearranged at any time without cost
- [x] Because it exchanges material, information and energy flows with its environment and thus has interfaces to other systems
- [ ] Because every element inside it is unrelated to the others
- [ ] Because it has no defined system boundary
> Explanation: In the systems approach a system consists of interrelated elements/subsystems whose functional relationships form the system structure. A factory maintains relationships with its environment (material, information, energy flows; market interfaces), making it an open system and part of a larger system. The approach structures information rather than delivering the solution itself.

### What distinguishes the analytical from the synthetic approach in factory planning?
- [ ] Analytical planning goes from fine to coarse (inside to outside); synthetic from coarse to fine (outside to inside)
- [x] Analytical planning proceeds from coarse to fine (outside to inside); synthetic planning proceeds from fine to coarse (inside to outside)
- [ ] Both approaches are strictly sequential and are never combined in one project
- [ ] The choice between them is arbitrary and independent of planning constraints
> Explanation: Analytical = outside-to-inside / coarse-to-fine; synthetic = inside-to-outside / fine-to-coarse. In real projects both alternate depending on constraints (given machinery, given plot), and the process is often run twice: factory level → detailed layout → back to factory level.

### According to the planning principles, why is "ideal planning" carried out even though real constraints exist?
- [ ] Because ideal plans are always cheaper to implement than realistic ones
- [x] Because applying ideal planning without constraints helps to identify deficiencies in the real solution
- [ ] Because constraints can be ignored in practice once an ideal plan exists
- [ ] Because ideal planning removes the need to generate variants
> Explanation: The "necessity of ideal planning" principle states that planning without constraints reveals weaknesses in the constrained real solution — it serves as a benchmark. Other principles include holistic, gradual (with overlaps/regressions), variant generation, economic, and interdisciplinary planning.

### How is continuous factory planning best described?
- [ ] As a one-time project that ends when production starts
- [x] As a control loop comparing target and actual state, deriving the need for change, and implementing planning measures continuously
- [ ] As a purely sequential process with no feedback from factory operation
- [ ] As replanning that is only allowed after the factory is fully depreciated
> Explanation: Continuous factory planning resolves the tension between turbulent markets and hard-to-revise location decisions. It works as a control loop: initial planning sets the target state, sensors record the actual state (key figures, trend analysis), the target-actual gap reveals the need for change, and the controller (continuous operational planning) triggers measures via the actuator — increasingly self-adapting under Industry 4.0.
