# Fabrikplanung · VL 02 – Goal definition

### What is the primary purpose of goal planning (goal definition) at the start of a factory planning project?
- [ ] To produce the detailed layout of the shop floor
- [x] To assess the feasibility of the project and concretize the task, resulting in a strategic concept that frames the subsequent planning
- [ ] To select the machinery and procure the equipment
- [ ] To measure the actual state of the running factory with sensors
> Explanation: Goal planning precedes all other stages. It analyzes the initial situation and defines requirements, delivering a strategic concept (the framework/task) for the subsequent factory planning — not yet any layout or equipment decisions.

### What distinguishes greenfield from brownfield planning?
- [x] Greenfield builds a new facility with minimal/no restrictions; brownfield transforms an already-built facility
- [ ] Greenfield transforms an existing facility; brownfield builds a completely new one
- [ ] Greenfield refers to sustainable factories; brownfield to conventional ones
- [ ] Greenfield concerns layout planning; brownfield concerns logistics planning
> Explanation: Greenfield = new planning with few constraints (e.g. Tesla Grünheide). Brownfield = re-planning of an existing site (e.g. Porsche Leipzig expansion). Because of shorter product life cycles, factory planning increasingly shifts toward continuous brownfield re-planning.

### Which of the following are forms of re-planning (brownfield)? (Mehrfachauswahl)
- [x] Expansion planning and structural renewal planning
- [x] Reduction planning and relocation planning
- [x] Outsourcing planning and optimization planning
- [ ] Greenfield planning for accessing an entirely new market
> Explanation: Brownfield/re-planning covers expansion, structural renewal, reduction, relocation, outsourcing and optimization planning. Accessing a new market with a new facility is a classic case of greenfield (new) planning, not re-planning.

### In which situation is genuinely new (greenfield) planning typically necessary rather than re-planning?
- [ ] When only the production quantities of existing products change slightly
- [x] When accessing new markets, or when outdated/uneconomical facilities no longer allow rationalization, expansion or re-planning
- [ ] Whenever a legal fire-protection requirement changes
- [ ] Whenever a customer requests a minor product modification
> Explanation: New planning is needed for new markets, when facilities are so outdated/uneconomical that re-planning is impossible, when rationalization/expansion is no longer feasible, or under inadequate working conditions. Minor quantity, legal or product changes are usually handled via brownfield measures.

### The impulses that initiate goal-definition measures are grouped into external and internal ones. Which is an INTERNAL (company) impulse?
- [ ] New competitors entering the market
- [ ] New legal environmental regulations
- [x] Productivity targets, procedural limitations, structural restrictions or complexity limits
- [ ] Changes in the manufacturing depth of the supply chain
> Explanation: The five impulse sources are Customer, Laws, Market and Supply Chain (external) and Company (internal). Internal company impulses include productivity targets, procedural limitations, structural restrictions and complexity limits.

### What are the three main steps of the goal-definition process, in order?
- [x] 1) Analyze the initial situation, 2) Define goal alternatives, 3) Select a goal alternative
- [ ] 1) Define goal alternatives, 2) Analyze the initial situation, 3) Select a goal alternative
- [ ] 1) Select a goal alternative, 2) Analyze the initial situation, 3) Define alternatives
- [ ] 1) Analyze the initial situation, 2) Build the detailed layout, 3) Procure equipment
> Explanation: First the initial situation (sales market and production program, forecasts) is analyzed, then goal alternatives responding to the requirements are defined, and finally one alternative is selected based on defined evaluation principles, determining the corporate strategy.

### What does an ABC analysis of the production program achieve?
- [x] It classifies items by their cumulative share of value/expenditure, so that few high-value A-items account for the largest share of cumulative annual expenditure
- [ ] It compares product types (P) directly against the number of assembly stations
- [ ] It ranks locations by their labor-cost advantage
- [ ] It measures the daily takt required to satisfy customer demand
> Explanation: The ABC analysis ranks items by cumulative value share: a small share of A-items causes the largest share of cumulative annual expenditure, while many C-items contribute little. The PQ analysis, by contrast, relates product types (P) to produced quantities (Q) and the revenue/profit generated.

### Which statement about the production program is correct?
- [ ] It is a purely short-term, daily schedule of machine assignments
- [x] It defines which product types are produced in which periods and quantities, and is oriented to the long term with a 3–5 year forecast
- [ ] It only lists the machines available in the factory
- [ ] It is identical to the detailed factory layout
> Explanation: The production program specifies product types, time periods and quantities. It is long-term oriented and includes a forecast for the next three to five years, forming the basis for deriving the performance requirement of the (re)designed production system.

### The current production potential of a factory is assessed with descriptive indicators. Which grouping matches the lecture?
- [x] Company, personnel, area, production and value-creation indicators, compared via internal/external benchmarking
- [ ] Only financial indicators such as capital structure and liquidity
- [ ] Only qualitative indicators such as image and job preservation
- [ ] Only the number of product variants and their colours
> Explanation: Production potential is described by company (revenue, market share), personnel (workforce, revenue per capita), area (land/production area), production (units/year, lead times, inventory) and value-creation indicators. These are compared through benchmarking and extended by trend analyses/scenario planning to derive required adaptability.

### A company keeps its existing products but wants to sell them in a new market segment. Which market/sales strategy does this correspond to?
- [ ] Intensification
- [x] Market expansion
- [ ] Product modification
- [ ] Diversification
> Explanation: Along the two axes (existing/new product × existing/new market): intensification = existing product in the old segment; market expansion = existing product in a new segment; product modification = new product in the old segment; diversification = new product in a new segment.

### Why does factory structuring aim to create "factories within the factory" (sub-factories)?
- [ ] To increase the number of management layers
- [x] Because opposing requirements (e.g. mass, series and custom products differing in flexibility and automation) cannot be optimally met by a single structure, so units with homogeneous requirements are formed
- [ ] To ensure every product is manufactured with the identical automation level
- [ ] Because legal regulations forbid mixing product types in one hall
> Explanation: When the production program mixes mass, series and custom products, their conflicting demands on flexibility and automation cannot be optimally realized in one structure. Sub-factories with requirements as homogeneous as possible are derived (function-, operation- or product-oriented) so each can be tailored specifically.

### When evaluating goal alternatives, which criteria are QUALITATIVE (not quantitative)?
- [ ] Investment costs, one-off costs and running costs
- [x] Degree of fulfillment of corporate goals, feasibility/complexity of implementation and realization risks
- [ ] Payback period and profitability
- [ ] Construction and machine acquisition costs
> Explanation: Quantitative criteria are the economic ones (investment, one-off and running costs, assessed via profitability or payback period). Qualitative criteria are the degree of goal fulfillment, feasibility/complexity and implementation risks (rated low/medium/high). Combined methods include AHP and the Ghandforoush multicriteria model.
