# Fabrikplanung · VL 08 – Factory Planning Management

### When is a utility analysis (Nutzwertanalyse) used, and how is the utility value determined?
- [x] For soft, monetarily hard-to-quantify criteria: build a hierarchical goal system, weight the criteria, normalize the scores onto one scale, then compute each alternative's utility value as the sum of (score × weight); the highest utility value wins
- [ ] Only for purely monetary criteria, by summing discounted cash flows
- [ ] By selecting the alternative with the lowest investment cost regardless of benefit
- [ ] By comparing internal rates of return of the alternatives
> Explanation: Utility analysis prepares decisions when soft criteria (e.g. flexibility, delivery readiness) matter. A hierarchical goal system is set up, criteria are weighted, differing rating scales are normalized (e.g. 1–10), and partial utilities (score × weight) are summed into a utility value per alternative; the highest value decides.

### What distinguishes the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) by Saaty from a plain utility analysis?
- [x] It weights criteria via pairwise comparison on a defined scale, computes weights with the eigenvector method, and provides an inconsistency factor measuring the logical consistency of the subjective judgments
- [ ] It ignores criteria weights entirely
- [ ] It only works for monetary criteria
- [ ] It replaces all human judgment with market prices
> Explanation: AHP is a mathematically more demanding variant of utility analysis. Criteria (and then alternatives per criterion) are compared pairwise on Saaty's scale; weights come from the eigenvector method (square matrix, row sums, normalize, repeat until stable). Its inconsistency factor reveals how contradictory the subjective evaluations are.

### In the Ghandforoush multi-criteria model, what is the role of a "critical factor" (CFM)?
- [x] It is an exclusion criterion rated 0 or 1: a CFM of 0 sets the alternative's overall utility PM to zero, removing it from consideration
- [ ] It is the monetary investment cost of the alternative
- [ ] It is the subjective weighting of flexibility
- [ ] It doubles the weight of the objective factors
> Explanation: Ghandforoush combines objective (monetary, OFM), subjective (SFM) and critical factors (CFM). The overall measure is PMᵢ = CFMᵢ · (X·OFMᵢ + (1−X)·SFMᵢ). Because CFM ∈ {0,1}, a failing critical factor (0) zeroes the whole PM — an exclusion/knock-out criterion (e.g. productivity must exceed 80%).

### What fundamentally distinguishes static from dynamic investment-appraisal methods?
- [x] Static methods take a time-point perspective on a single representative period and ignore the time value of money; dynamic methods evaluate cash flows over the whole useful life and discount them (time preference)
- [ ] Static methods discount cash flows; dynamic methods do not
- [ ] Static methods always give more accurate results than dynamic ones
- [ ] Both consider the entire life cycle identically
> Explanation: Static methods (cost/profit comparison, profitability, static payback) use practical cost/revenue figures for one representative period, so no discounting is needed. Dynamic methods (NPV, IRR, complete financial plan, life-cycle costing) capture the full useful life and discount inflows/outflows to reflect time preference.

### A machine costs €200,000, has a 10-year useful life (linear depreciation, no residual value) and an imputed interest rate of 8%. What are its imputed depreciation and imputed interest per year in the cost comparison method?
- [x] Depreciation €20,000/a and imputed interest €8,000/a (interest on average tied-up capital = 200,000/2 × 0.08)
- [ ] Depreciation €20,000/a and imputed interest €16,000/a (interest on the full acquisition cost)
- [ ] Depreciation €2,000/a and imputed interest €8,000/a
- [ ] Depreciation €20,000/a and imputed interest €4,000/a
> Explanation: Linear depreciation = acquisition cost / useful life = 200,000/10 = €20,000/a. Imputed interest is charged on the average tied-up capital (acquisition cost / 2): 200,000/2 × 0.08 = 100,000 × 0.08 = €8,000/a. The cost comparison uses period costs at equal output, unit costs at different output.

### A machining center makes 140 parts/h at €70/h labour; conventional production uses 4 workers (200 parts/h) at €45/worker·h. Material is €0.30/part for both. Comparing unit (variable) costs, which is cheaper?
- [x] The machining center at €0.80/part (70/140 + 0.30) vs. conventional €1.20/part (180/200 + 0.30)
- [ ] Conventional at €0.90/part, cheaper than the machining center
- [ ] Both are equal at €1.20/part
- [ ] The machining center at €0.50/part vs. conventional €0.90/part (material ignored)
> Explanation: Because outputs differ, compare unit costs. Machining center: labour 70/140 = €0.50 + material €0.30 = €0.80/part. Conventional: labour (4×45)/200 = 180/200 = €0.90 + material €0.30 = €1.20/part. The machining center has the lower unit cost. (Selling price €1.50 is equal, so the cost comparison suffices.)

### Fixed costs are €120,000/period, the selling price is €50/unit and variable costs are €30/unit. What is the break-even quantity?
- [x] 6,000 units (120,000 / (50 − 30))
- [ ] 2,400 units (120,000 / 50)
- [ ] 4,000 units (120,000 / 30)
- [ ] 3,000 units
> Explanation: Break-even quantity = fixed costs / (price − variable cost per unit) = 120,000 / (50 − 30) = 120,000 / 20 = 6,000 units. At this output revenue equals total costs (neither profit nor loss); the profit-comparison method favours the alternative with higher profit or lower break-even point.

### An investment of €90,000 yields an average profit of €12,000/a and annual depreciation of €18,000. What is the static payback (amortization) period?
- [x] 3 years (90,000 / (12,000 + 18,000))
- [ ] 7.5 years (90,000 / 12,000)
- [ ] 5 years (90,000 / 18,000)
- [ ] 4.5 years
> Explanation: Static payback = initial investment / (profit per year + depreciation per year) = 90,000 / (12,000 + 18,000) = 90,000 / 30,000 = 3 years. The method assesses financial risk (how fast capital returns); its weakness is ignoring the remaining useful life and profitability beyond payback.

### An investment costs €10,000 (t=0) and returns €6,000 at t=1 and €6,600 at t=2, at a discount rate i = 10%. What is the net present value (NPV), and is it advantageous?
- [x] NPV ≈ +€909, so it is advantageous (NPV > 0)
- [ ] NPV ≈ −€909, so it is not advantageous
- [ ] NPV = +€2,600 (undiscounted sum minus investment)
- [ ] NPV = €0, decision is indifferent
> Explanation: NPV = Σ(Eₜ − Aₜ)/(1+i)ᵗ = −10,000 + 6,000/1.1 + 6,600/1.21 = −10,000 + 5,454.5 + 5,454.5 ≈ +€909. A positive NPV means the project earns more than the discount rate, so it is advantageous; among alternatives, the highest positive NPV is chosen.

### How is the internal rate of return (IRR) defined and interpreted?
- [x] The discount rate r at which the NPV equals zero; the investment is advantageous if r > i (required rate), indifferent if r = i, unfavourable if r < i
- [ ] The rate at which the NPV is maximized
- [ ] The static payback period expressed as a percentage
- [ ] The ratio of fixed to variable costs
> Explanation: The IRR is the calculation interest rate that makes the NPV zero — the average return on the capital tied up in the object. It is compared with the company's minimum required return i: r > i advantageous, r = i indifferent, r < i unfavourable. Direct solving is only possible for one/two periods or equal payments; otherwise approximation/graphical methods are used.

### What is the aim of Life-Cycle Costing (LCC, VDI 2884), and how are costs made comparable?
- [x] To consider all costs and revenues over the entire life cycle (before, during and after usage), so a higher purchase price can still be the cheaper option overall; costs are discounted/uplifted to a common reference point (capital-value method)
- [ ] To consider only the acquisition cost of a machine
- [ ] To ignore operating and disposal costs
- [ ] To maximize the number of variants produced
> Explanation: LCC evaluates the total cost of ownership across initiation, planning, engineering/realization, operation (incl. maintenance/debt service) and disposal. A holistic view can justify a higher purchase price if lifetime costs are lower. All costs are discounted to a common point using the capital-value (NPV) method; the alternative with the lowest life-cycle cost is chosen.

### Which three scenario types does the scenario technique distinguish?
- [x] Positive extreme ("wish") scenario, negative extreme ("horror") scenario, and trend scenario (present extended into the future, judged most probable)
- [ ] Best case, average case and worst case, all equally probable
- [ ] Optimistic, realistic and random scenarios
- [ ] Only a single most-likely scenario
> Explanation: A scenario describes a conceivable future situation and its development path. The positive extreme models the best case (wish scenario, possible but unlikely), the negative extreme the worst case (horror scenario), and the trend scenario extends today's situation forward — assumed more probable than the two extremes. Scenarios help formulate and test strategy alternatives.

### The "real options" approach (Myers, 1977) treats investments like financial options. Which three properties underlie the analogy?
- [x] Flexibility (the choice whether to exercise), uncertainty (value depends on the uncertain underlying, e.g. demand) and irreversibility (exercise/expiry is final and the price paid is lost)
- [ ] Liquidity, solvency and profitability
- [ ] Depreciation, interest and residual value
- [ ] Standardization, modularity and scalability
> Explanation: Myers noted investments share option-like properties: flexibility (management can choose to exercise a future opportunity), uncertainty (its value depends on an uncertain underlying such as future demand) and irreversibility (the initial outlay is sunk and the action cannot be undone) — e.g. building an oversized hall that later can be filled with further investment. The main value lies in "options thinking" and valuing strategic flexibility.

### Match the project organization form to its description. Which pairings are correct? (Mehrfachauswahl)
- [x] Staff (influence) project organization: the project manager coordinates without personnel authority — low effort but low acceptance; only for very small projects
- [x] Matrix project organization: line provides resources/expertise, project management owns goals/coordination with defined rights — suited to medium/large multidisciplinary projects, but risk of conflict
- [x] Project-based organization: the project manager has full disciplinary responsibility for all resources; team detached from the line — for high-risk projects, but costly team setup/teardown
- [ ] Staff project organization gives the project manager full disciplinary authority over all resources
> Explanation: Staff/influence organization = coordination without personnel authority (low effort, low acceptance). Matrix = overlay of line and project management with shared responsibility (flexible, but conflict-prone). Project-based (autonomous) = the project manager has full authority and a dedicated team, best for risky projects but with setup/dismantling effort. Full authority belongs to the project-based form, not the staff form.

### What are the tasks of project controlling, and how is a deviation handled?
- [x] Monitoring implementation against objectives: gather hard and soft data, compare target vs. actual, run root-cause analysis on deviations and derive corrective measures; project control additionally releases work packages and reacts to disruptions
- [ ] Only defining the project goals at the very start
- [ ] Only motivating the team, without measuring progress
- [ ] Replacing milestones with a single final review
> Explanation: Project controlling covers planning, monitoring and control. Monitoring gathers hard data (cost, deadlines, capacity) and soft data (problems, sentiment), performs target/actual comparison, root-cause analysis and reporting (methods: milestone/cost trend analysis, earned value, KPIs). Project control releases work packages, decides on partial results and initiates responses to disruptions. Each project phase closes with a verifiable, formally approved milestone.
